Dynamic Modeling and Derivation of a Precision Ball Screw Drive Stage

Revision 2. Linear-domain formulation. Supersedes the rotational-domain 2-DOF derivation.

How to Use This Document as a Model-Build Specification

This document is the authoritative topology and assumption record. Build or review the model in the following order:

  1. Use the physical chain and coordinate definitions in Section 1; do not introduce a rigid \(x_d=x_s\) constraint because it removes the nut-friction identification channel.
  2. Apply every modeling assumption in Section 2 and preserve the measured/derived/placeholder status of each parameter.
  3. Choose the magnetic-force switches in Section 3. Use the linear law for transfer functions and modes, and the sinusoidal law for bounded time-domain stepping.
  4. Assemble the two body equations exactly as derived in Section 4. In particular, nut friction must have opposite signs on the two bodies.
  5. Use Section 6 to select either LuGre or GMS independently at each active site, then use Section 7 to activate Case 0/A/B/C/D.
  6. Check the requested measurement topology against Section 8 before claiming that individual friction sites are identifiable.
  7. Carry every limitation in Section 9 into result captions and conclusions.

The full algebra, state augmentation, case-by-case equations, Bode responses, bounded stepping response, and tracking error are in the rendered companion Analytical Derivation and Response Study. Both browser pages and all figures are regenerated by the single-purpose command:

python .\build_model_documentation.py
Parameter-status rule. A placeholder or provisional response parameter may be used to exercise model structure, but it must not be presented as an identified prediction of the hardware.

1. System Layout and Coordinate Choice

1.1 Physical chain

   [Commanded field position: x_cmd]
                  |
        (Magnetic spring: F_mag, nonlinear)
                  v
   [Node 1: Drivetrain  m_d , x_d ]  ---> [bearing friction to ground]
        rotor + bellows + screw, lumped
                  |
        (Axial compliance: k_ax , c_ax)
        (Nut friction acts IN PARALLEL here)
                  v
   [Node 2: Stage  m_s , x_s ]  ---> [guideway friction to ground]
        table + ball nut + 4 carriages + payload

1.2 Mechanical degrees of freedom and kinematic diagram

The mechanical model has exactly two degrees of freedom: \(x_d\) and \(x_s\). The field command \(x_{cmd}\) is an imposed input, not a degree of freedom. LuGre bristle deflections and GMS element forces are internal friction states; they enlarge the first-order state vector but do not add independent rigid-body motion coordinates. Rotor angle is already represented by \(x_d=r\theta_d\) and is therefore not a third DOF.

1.3 Why the linear domain

The transmission ratio is defined as

\[r = \frac{L}{2\pi} \qquad [\text{m/rad}]\]

with \(L\) the screw lead. Displacements map as \(x = r\theta\) and forces map as \(F = T/r\). Working in the linear domain gives three concrete advantages.

  1. Both friction sites are expressed in newtons, so their contributions are directly comparable and directly summable. In the rotational domain the nut friction requires a bristle stiffness in N·m/rad, which is a bookkeeping artifact rather than a physical quantity.
  2. The measurement chain is linear. The interferometer reads metres, and the flexure force sensing reads newtons.
  3. Published GMS and LuGre parameters for linear guideways and ball nuts are given in N, N/m and N·s/m.

1.4 Why the axial compliance is the correct internal DOF

Reflecting a torsional stiffness to the linear domain divides by \(r^2\). With \(L = 1\) mm, \(r = 1.592\times10^{-4}\) m/rad and \(r^2 = 2.533\times10^{-8}\). This is a very small number, so the reflection direction dominates the result.

Two consequences follow.

The inertia ratio is \(m_d/m_s \approx 98\). The drivetrain is effectively immovable as seen from the stage. This is why the impact-hammer identification returned a clean fixed-base effective mass of 0.60 kg.

The stiffness ordering is \(\sigma_{0,g} \ll k_{ax} < K_m \ll k_c/r^2\). The bellows is the stiffest element in the series chain by two orders of magnitude, and the axial path is the second softest. Revision 1 retained the stiffest element as its flexible degree of freedom and declared the second softest rigid.

1.5 Justification for lumping the bellows rigid

Series compliance argument. The bellows contributes \(1/(4.0\times10^9)\) against \(1/(1.14\times10^7)\) for the axial path. Its share of the total drivetrain series compliance is below 0.3 percent.

Mode separation argument. The discarded rotor-against-screw torsional mode sits at

\[\omega_{tors} = \sqrt{k_c\left(\frac{1}{J_m} + \frac{1}{J_s}\right)} \approx 2.04\times10^{4}\ \text{rad/s} \approx 3.2\ \text{kHz}\]

which is well above the ≤900 Hz band of interest and above the significant microstep excitation content.

Open item. The mode-separation argument depends on \(k_c\), which is currently a placeholder. At \(k_c = 10\) N·m/rad the discarded mode falls to approximately 1.0 kHz and the argument weakens materially. The bellows torsional stiffness must be taken from the coupling datasheet and \(\omega_{tors}(k_c)\) reported as a sensitivity.

2. Modeling Assumptions

  • Lumped drivetrain body. Rotor, bellows coupling and screw translate as one reflected mass \(m_d\). Justified in Section 1.5.
  • Rigid stage body. Table plate, ball nut and four carriages form one rigid mass \(m_s\). Carriage skirt and rail bending compliance are omitted.
  • Single axial compliance. Screw shaft axial stiffness, support bearing axial stiffness and nut body stiffness are lumped into one spring \(k_{ax}\) acting between \(m_d\) and \(m_s\).
  • \(k_{ax}\) is the sliding-regime structural stiffness. It excludes presliding ball-contact compliance, which is carried by the friction model instead. See the double-counting note in Section 6.3.
  • Position-independent parameters. \(k_{ax}\) varies with carriage position because the free screw length changes. This is treated as a documented limitation, not an additional degree of freedom.
  • Ideal kinematic transform. The screw-nut transform is taken as \(x = r\theta\) with unit efficiency. Transmission losses are represented explicitly by the nut friction element rather than by an efficiency factor \(\eta\).
  • Damping retained. Structural damping \(c_{ax}\), electromagnetic drivetrain damping \(c_m\), and viscous friction \(\sigma_2\) are carried through the response model and set to zero only for the energy-conserving modal baseline of Section 5. \(c_m\) represents current-regulator/back-EMF damping and remains an unidentified, highlighted input.

3. The Drive Force

3.1 Nonlinear magnetic spring

For a two-phase hybrid stepper with \(N_r\) rotor teeth, the commutation torque about the commanded equilibrium is

\[T_{mag} = T_{max}\sin\big(N_r(\theta_{cmd} - \theta_d)\big)\]

Converting to the linear domain with \(F = T/r\) and \(\theta = x/r\):

\[\boxed{\;F_{comm} = F_{max}\sin\big(\kappa\,(x_{cmd} - x_d)\big)\;}\]

where

\[F_{max} = \frac{T_{max}}{r}, \qquad \kappa = \frac{N_r}{r} = \frac{2\pi N_r}{L} \quad [\text{rad/m}]\]

\(\kappa\) is the spatial electrical wavenumber. One full step corresponds to an electrical quarter cycle, so the full-step pitch is \(L/(4N_r)\).

3.2 Linearized magnetic spring

Expanding about zero lag angle:

\[\boxed{\;F_{comm} \approx K_m\,(x_{cmd} - x_d), \qquad K_m = F_{max}\kappa = \frac{T_{max}N_r}{r^2} = \frac{k_m}{r^2}\;}\]

which recovers \(k_m = N_r T_{max}\) as the rotational-domain equivalent.

3.3 Electromagnetic drivetrain damping

An ideal magnetic spring with an instantaneous field-position step stores and returns energy but cannot dissipate it. The original model therefore leaves the 226 Hz common-motion mode almost undamped: \(c_{ax}\) acts only on \(\dot x_d-\dot x_s\), which is small in that mode. The response model includes the missing velocity-dependent drive term

\[\boxed{F_{em,damp}=-c_m\dot x_d}.\]

For a transparent provisional parameterization,

\[c_m=2\zeta_m\sqrt{K_m m_d},\]

where \(\zeta_m\) is the assumed damping ratio of the isolated reflected drivetrain-on-magnetic-spring mode. This does not change the undamped modal frequencies. It changes only the damped Bode and stepping responses.

3.4 Detent torque

Detent torque is referenced to the rotor's absolute position rather than to the lag angle, and for a two-phase hybrid stepper it completes four cycles per electrical cycle:

\[F_{det} = -\,\frac{T_{det}}{r}\sin\big(4\kappa\,x_d\big)\]

3.5 Switchable combined form

Two binary switches select the model variant. \(\lambda_{mag} \in \{0,1\}\) selects linearized or nonlinear commutation. \(\lambda_{det} \in \{0,1\}\) enables detent.

\[F_{drive} = (1-\lambda_{mag})\,K_m(x_{cmd}-x_d) \;+\; \lambda_{mag}\,F_{max}\sin\!\big(\kappa(x_{cmd}-x_d)\big) \;-\; c_m\dot x_d \;-\; \lambda_{det}\,\frac{T_{det}}{r}\sin\!\big(4\kappa\,x_d\big)\]
\(\lambda_{mag}\)\(\lambda_{det}\)Use
00Modal analysis, transfer functions, eigenvalue extraction
10Nonlinear step response, large-lag transients, pull-out margin
11Full simulation, microstep monotonicity, detent-induced position error

3.6 Command discretization

\(x_{cmd}\) is not continuous. It is a staircase set by the microstep index \(n \in \mathbb{Z}\) and the microstep divisor \(\mu\):

\[x_{cmd}(t) = \frac{L}{4N_r\,\mu}\,n(t)\]

The continuous treatment used in Section 5 is a modal-analysis convenience only. All time-domain simulation must use the staircase.

4. Derivation of the Equations of Motion

Step 1 — Lump the drivetrain mass

Rotor, coupling and screw are rigidly connected under the Section 1.5 assumption. Reflecting their combined inertia through the screw transform:

\[m_d = \frac{J_m + J_c + J_s}{r^2}\]

Step 2 — Lump the stage mass

\[m_s = m_{stage} + m_{nut} + 4\,m_{cart} + m_{payload}\]

Step 3 — Define the three friction sites and their driving velocities

SiteSymbolDriving velocityActs between
Linear guideways\(F_{f,g}\)\(v_g = \dot x_s\)stage and ground
Ball nut and screw\(F_{f,n}\)\(v_n = \dot x_d - \dot x_s\)drivetrain and stage
Support and motor bearings\(F_{f,d}\)\(v_d = \dot x_d\)drivetrain and ground

Each \(F_{f,\alpha}\) is a function of its own velocity and its own internal states. Each is defined positive when opposing positive \(v_\alpha\).

Step 4 — Drivetrain free body diagram

The drivetrain is driven by the magnetic force, resisted by the axial spring and damper, by the nut friction reaction and by its own bearing friction:

\[m_d\ddot x_d = F_{mag} - c_m\dot x_d - k_{ax}(x_d - x_s) - c_{ax}(\dot x_d - \dot x_s) - F_{f,n} - F_{f,d}\]

Step 5 — Stage free body diagram

The stage is driven forward by the axial spring and damper and by the nut friction reaction, and resisted by the guideway friction:

\[m_s\ddot x_s = k_{ax}(x_d - x_s) + c_{ax}(\dot x_d - \dot x_s) + F_{f,n} - F_{f,g}\]

The sign of \(F_{f,n}\) is opposite in the two equations because it is an internal interaction force. When the drivetrain leads the stage, \(v_n > 0\), the nut friction retards the drivetrain and drags the stage forward.

Step 6 — Assemble

\[\begin{bmatrix} m_d & 0 \\ 0 & m_s \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \ddot x_d \\ \ddot x_s \end{bmatrix} + c_{ax}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \dot x_d \\ \dot x_s \end{bmatrix} + c_m\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \dot x_d \\ \dot x_s \end{bmatrix} + k_{ax}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_d \\ x_s \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} F_{mag} - F_{f,n} - F_{f,d} \\[2pt] F_{f,n} - F_{f,g} \end{bmatrix}\]

This is the master equation. Note that the stiffness matrix alone is singular. Grounding is supplied entirely by \(F_{mag}\), which depends on \(x_d\).

5. Linearized Modal Baseline

Set \(\lambda_{mag} = 0\), \(\lambda_{det} = 0\), \(c_{ax} = 0\) and all friction to zero. Moving \(K_m x_d\) to the left-hand side gives the standard grounded form:

\[\begin{bmatrix} m_d & 0 \\ 0 & m_s \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \ddot x_d \\ \ddot x_s \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} K_m + k_{ax} & -k_{ax} \\ -k_{ax} & k_{ax} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_d \\ x_s \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} K_m x_{cmd} \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\]

The characteristic equation is

\[m_d m_s\,\omega^4 - \big[m_d k_{ax} + m_s(K_m + k_{ax})\big]\omega^2 + K_m k_{ax} = 0\]

5.1 Numerical check against measurement

Substituting \(m_d = 59\) kg, \(m_s = 0.60\) kg, \(K_m = 1.2\times10^8\) N/m, \(k_{ax} = 1.14\times10^7\) N/m:

ModePredictedIndependent evidenceShape
1226 Hz239 Hz, analytical rotor-in-detentDrivetrain on magnetic spring, stage riding along

Both predicted modes correspond to independently observed features. The 226 versus 239 Hz gap is attributable to the placeholder \(J_m\) and should close once the rotor inertia is either taken from the datasheet or measured by free deceleration.

For comparison, the Revision 1 model predicts 240 Hz and 3.2 kHz. It reproduces the detent mode, invents a mode at 3.2 kHz that is dynamically irrelevant here, and cannot reproduce the 620–690 Hz stage mode at all.

5.2 Limiting forms

Because \(m_d \gg m_s\) and \(K_m > k_{ax}\), the two modes decouple to good approximation:

\[\omega_1 \approx \sqrt{\frac{K_m}{m_d}}, \qquad \omega_2 \approx \sqrt{\frac{k_{ax}}{m_s}}\]

giving 227 Hz and 694 Hz. The stage mode is a fixed-base mode to within one percent.

6. Friction Models

Both models are given in identical notation so that the forcing terms of Section 7 are interchangeable between them. Everything below applies independently at each of the three sites \(\alpha \in \{g, n, d\}\), with \(v\) standing for the site's driving velocity \(v_\alpha\) from Step 3.

6.0 Shared Stribeck attractor

\[s(v) = F_c + (F_s - F_c)\exp\!\left(-\left|\frac{v}{v_s}\right|^{\delta}\right)\]

with \(F_s\) the breakaway force, \(F_c\) the Coulomb force, \(v_s\) the Stribeck velocity and \(\delta\) the shape exponent. Both models use this same function.

6.1 Generalized Maxwell-Slip (GMS)

GMS is a parallel bank of \(N\) elastoplastic elements. Element \(i\) carries its own force state \(F_i\), its own stiffness \(k_i\) and a normalized weight \(\nu_i\) with \(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nu_i = 1\). Each element is independently either stuck or slipping, so the model is a switched hybrid system with \(2^N\) discrete modes.

Stuck:

\[\dot F_i = k_i\,v \qquad \text{valid while } |F_i| < \nu_i\,s(v)\]

Transition to slipping occurs when \(|F_i|\) reaches \(\nu_i s(v)\).

Slipping:

\[\boxed{\dot F_i = C\left(\operatorname{sgn}(v) - \frac{F_i}{\nu_i\,s(v)}\right)}\]

This signed-attractor form is essential: its stable sliding equilibrium is \(F_i=\operatorname{sgn}(v)\nu_i s(v)\). The previously written product form, \(\operatorname{sgn}(v)C[1-F_i/(\nu_i s)]\), has the wrong equilibrium during negative sliding and drives a negative force away from its threshold.

Transition back to stuck occurs at velocity reversal, that is when \(v\) changes sign or reaches zero.

Total friction force:

\[F_{f} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} F_i \;+\; \sigma_2\,v\]

Two aggregate quantities connect this to the lumped description:

\[\sigma_0 = \sum_{i=1}^{N} k_i \qquad \text{(presliding stiffness at zero deflection)}\]
\[\left.\sum_i \nu_i s(v)\right|_{v \to 0} = F_s \qquad \text{(total breakaway force)}\]

Because the elements reach their individual thresholds at different deflections, the model retains non-local memory: the current force depends on the full history of reversals, not only on the present state. This is the property that motivates GMS over single-state models and it is not reproducible with any single internal state.

6.2 LuGre and its elastoplastic extension

LuGre carries one internal state \(z\), interpreted as an average bristle deflection.

State equation:

\[\dot z = v - \sigma_0\frac{|v|}{s(v)}\,z\]

Force output:

\[F_{f} = \sigma_0 z + \sigma_1\dot z + \sigma_2 v\]

The elastoplastic extension introduces a transition function \(\alpha(z,v) \in [0,1]\) to eliminate drift during true stick:

\[\dot z = v\left[1 - \alpha(z,v)\operatorname{sgn}(v)\frac{\sigma_0 z}{s(v)}\right]\]

Setting \(\alpha = 1\) recovers standard LuGre exactly.

Note on Revision 1. The state equation given in the Revision 1 appendix, expanded, is \(\dot z = v - |v|\sigma_0 z/F_{ss}(v)\). That is LuGre, not GMS. The two models were conflated.

6.4 Double-counting caveat

The Hertzian ball-raceway contact compliance appears in two places. It is physically part of \(k_{ax}\), and it is also part of the nut GMS bank's \(\sigma_{0,n} = \sum k_i\). To avoid counting it twice, \(k_{ax}\) is defined as the sliding-regime structural stiffness, measured with the friction bristles saturated. The impact-hammer identification is performed at gross-sliding amplitude, so the measured \(1.14\times10^7\) N/m is consistent with this definition. Any \(k_{ax}\) obtained from a small-amplitude quasi-static test would not be, and would double-count.

7. Friction Site Variants

The master equation of Step 6 is unchanged in all cases. Only the active friction ports and their internal laws change. A/B/C use LuGre. A2/B2/C2 repeat the same physical ports with GMS so friction-law effects are never confused with topology effects. Case D remains the optional full architecture and is not part of the present paired response comparison.

Case 0 — Frictionless

\[F_{f,g} = F_{f,n} = F_{f,d} = 0, \qquad \mathbf{f} = \begin{bmatrix} F_{mag} \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\]

Frictionless baseline. For undamped modal extraction, set \(c_{ax}=c_m=0\); for Bode and stepping responses, retain the separately modeled structural and electromagnetic damping.

Case A — Guideway friction only

Friction acts between the stage and ground, driven by absolute stage velocity.

\[\mathbf{f} = \begin{bmatrix} F_{mag} \\[2pt] -\,F_{f,g}(\dot x_s,\ \mathbf{z}_g) \end{bmatrix}\]

This is the dominant site by presliding compliance. Directly observable by a stage-mounted interferometer.

Case A2 — Guideway friction only, GMS

The body equations and velocity \(v_g=\dot x_s\) are identical to Case A. Replace the single LuGre state \(z_g\) by the GMS force bank \(\{F_{i,g}\}_{i=1}^{N_g}\) and sum its output to obtain \(F_{f,g}\).

Case B — Nut and screw friction only

Friction acts across the screw-nut interface, driven by relative velocity, and therefore appears in parallel with \(k_{ax}\) with opposite signs in the two rows.

\[\mathbf{f} = \begin{bmatrix} -\,F_{f,n}(\dot x_d - \dot x_s,\ \mathbf{z}_n) \;+\; F_{mag} \\[2pt] +\,F_{f,n}(\dot x_d - \dot x_s,\ \mathbf{z}_n) \end{bmatrix}\]

Not observable from stage position alone. Manifests only as differential deflection across \(k_{ax}\).

Case B2 — Nut and screw friction only, GMS

The body equations and velocity \(v_n=\dot x_d-\dot x_s\) are identical to Case B. Replace \(z_n\) by the GMS force bank \(\{F_{i,n}\}_{i=1}^{N_n}\).

Case C — Guideway and nut friction

\[\mathbf{f} = \begin{bmatrix} F_{mag} - F_{f,n}(\dot x_d - \dot x_s,\ \mathbf{z}_n) \\[2pt] F_{f,n}(\dot x_d - \dot x_s,\ \mathbf{z}_n) - F_{f,g}(\dot x_s,\ \mathbf{z}_g) \end{bmatrix}\]

Two independent friction banks acting on two distinct velocity arguments. This is the minimum structure required for the parameter-transfer study.

Case C2 — Guideway and nut friction, GMS

The mechanical equations are identical to Case C. Both active LuGre states are replaced by independent GMS banks driven by \(v_g\) and \(v_n\) respectively.

Case D — Full model

Adds support-bearing and motor-bearing friction on the drivetrain node, driven by absolute drivetrain velocity.

\[\mathbf{f} = \begin{bmatrix} F_{mag} - F_{f,n}(\dot x_d - \dot x_s,\ \mathbf{z}_n) - F_{f,d}(\dot x_d,\ \mathbf{z}_d) \\[2pt] F_{f,n}(\dot x_d - \dot x_s,\ \mathbf{z}_n) - F_{f,g}(\dot x_s,\ \mathbf{z}_g) \end{bmatrix}\]

\(F_{f,d}\) is the site addressed by rotor spin-down and breakaway measurements, reflected as \(F_{f,d} = T_{brg}/r\).

8. Identifiability

The friction-site separation of Section 7 exists only because the axial degree of freedom was retained. This has a direct consequence for parameter identification.

Under a single-coordinate model, the rigid kinematic lock forces \(\dot x_d = \dot x_s\). Then \(v_n \equiv 0\) and \(v_g \equiv v_d\), so the guideway and drivetrain friction terms are driven by the same velocity and sum at the same node. Only the aggregate \(F_{f,g} + F_{f,d}\) is identifiable. The split is structurally unrecoverable regardless of measurement quality or excitation richness.

Under the two-coordinate model of Step 6, the three sites are driven by three distinct velocity arguments and act on two distinct bodies:

  • \(F_{f,g}\) sets the absolute lost motion of the stage and is visible to a stage-referenced measurement.
  • \(F_{f,n}\) appears only as differential deflection across \(k_{ax}\) and requires a differential measurement spanning the drivetrain and stage pickup points.
  • \(F_{f,d}\) acts on the drivetrain node against ground and is separable only if the drivetrain coordinate is instrumented.

This is the formal statement of why the instrumented testbed is necessary and why the differential interferometer architecture is the correct measurement topology. In the deployed open-loop configuration, where neither the drivetrain coordinate nor the differential deflection is measurable, only the aggregate is observable. The identification must therefore be performed on the testbed and the split transferred.

9. Known Limitations

  1. \(J_m\), \(J_c\), \(J_s\) and \(k_c\) are placeholders. Every number in Sections 1.3, 1.4 and 5.1 is provisional until they are measured or sourced.
  2. \(k_{ax}\) is position dependent through the varying free screw length. Treated as constant here.
  3. Carriage skirt compliance and rail bending are absorbed into the rigid stage assumption.
  4. Yaw, pitch and roll of the stage are not modeled. Only the drive axis is represented.
  5. Thermal dependence of all friction parameters is omitted.
  6. The screw transform is treated as ideal. Lead error, cyclic error and screw runout are not represented.
  7. Electrical dynamics of the stepper are omitted. Current is assumed to track command instantaneously, which is defensible below 900 RPM but not at higher rates.
  8. Electromagnetic damping \(c_m\) is required for a realistic transient but has not been identified. The response study highlights its assumed value and must be rerun after ring-down identification.

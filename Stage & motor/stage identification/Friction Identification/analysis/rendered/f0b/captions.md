# F0b results figures — captions

**F0b-1.** Measurement floor at rest. (a) Precision of a window mean against averaging time at the middle and both ends of the travel, as the overlapping Allan deviation after removing a linear trend from each hold; the dashed line is the white-noise slope. At the 0.5 s record window (open markers) the precision is 0.50 nm, giving a minimum detectable step of 2.1 nm, 19 times below one microstep. The floor does not depend on position: the three holds agree within 0.10 nm at 0.5 s. Averaging stops helping beyond about 0.2 s, where drift takes over at +4 nm/min at the middle of the travel.

**F0b-2.** Lost motion after a reversal against stroke, from the reversal ladder at $x_0$ = 60 mm. (a) Lost motion as commanded stroke minus measured travel between the settled window means at the two turning points; filled: increasing pass, open: decreasing pass (dodged right), grey diamonds: the first half-cycle of each stroke, which starts from whatever the previous stroke left rather than from a closed loop. Error bars are ±2σ over half-cycles. Small strokes lose almost all their motion (dashed diagonal); lost motion saturates at $\ell_{sat}$ = 2.30 µm, about 30 % above $\beta_0$ = 1.757 µm from E2–E5, with a pre-sliding length $s_{90}$ = 7.5 µm — two and a half times the 3 µm that F1 and F2 were planned on. The two passes agree to 126 nm, so the ladder carries no history beyond the stroke itself and F1 needs no extra wipes between strokes. (b) The same data as the fraction of the commanded stroke that reaches the carriage; the light line is a reading aid. Strokes above 100 µm are not shown: their rungs run at over 25 µm/s and do not settle within the 0.5 s turning-point pause.

**F0b-3.** Slow ramps at 0.25 µm/s after a reversal and in continuation over the same commanded positions, both repetitions of each. (a) Downward: reversal (S1, solid) and continuation (S4, dashed); their difference Δ (thick) isolates the friction transient, since the geometric and microstep error are identical on both. The carriage leaves the stuck line after 40 nm, about one microstep, but only half of the commanded motion is arriving by 1.4 µm and Δ has not flattened within 20 µm — consistent with the 7.5 µm pre-sliding length of F0b-2. (b) The same upward, deadband 55 nm. (c) The first 30 microsteps against time: the commanded staircase advances 39 nm every 156 ms, and the carriage follows it smoothly at a mean 20 nm per step after a reversal, with 0 % of steps delivering less than d_min and 0 % more than twice the mean. There is no sawtooth: the carriage does not stick and jump, it simply arrives short.

**F0b-4.** (a) Lost motion after a reversal following 1 s and 60 s at rest, two trials each in ABBA order; the band is ±d_min around the mean of the short-dwell trials. The difference at the end of the ramp is -40 nm against a trial-to-trial scatter of 66 nm, so although it exceeds the 2.1 nm detection limit it is not separable from the scatter on two repetitions: the probe is underpowered rather than null, and F2 is not decided by it. Inset: creep during the 60 s rest. (b) Friction lag during steady motion at 0.01 mm/s, taken as half the difference of the up and down passes at the same commanded position, which cancels the geometric error exactly: 0.70 µm, flat along the segment to ±0.10 µm and close to $eta_0/2$. The 1 mm/s passes are not comparable with it and are not drawn: reading a lag part-way through a move needs the commanded position at that instant, the segment log gives only start and end times, and the end timestamp carries up to 26 ms of overhead beyond the step train. The grey band is what that uncertainty alone is worth at 1 mm/s — tens of microns, larger than any friction effect. F0b therefore does not settle the speed question; F3 needs the step-train end logged, or the two speeds compared between settled endpoints rather than mid-move.

**F0b-A1.** Session validity. (a) Drift of the reference-pair mean at 15 mm (filled, solid) and 105 mm (open, dashed), each referred to its own first visit; averaging the up and down approaches removes the reversal error and leaves the slow change. The two ends move differently — 15 mm walks 0.8 µm over the session while 105 mm stays put — so the drift is a change in the stage, not in the instrument, and the correction is interpolated in position as well as in time. (b) Standard deviation of each 0.5 s record window; all are below the threshold once the 50 ms trailing guard is applied, without which three of the twelve reference windows reach 2.1–2.9 µm because the two clocks have slipped far enough by the end of the session for the next move to start inside the window. (c) MSCNT at every return to $x_0$: one value throughout, so no steps were lost. (d) Checks.

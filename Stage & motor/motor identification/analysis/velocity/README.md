# Block 6 - Velocity plateaus: friction against speed, and ripple magnification

Actions 46-67 of the 2026-10-13 identification run. Eleven constant-rate
plateaus from 0.25 to 400 full-step/s, each immediately repeated in reverse
over the same angles, all at MRES=16, sampled at 2 kHz. Every plateau starts at
its final rate with no ramp.

Generated by [`../../scripts/velocity_figures.py`](../../scripts/velocity_figures.py).
Carries over f_n = 153.0 Hz and zeta = 0.0528 from block 5,
and the static-map harmonic amplitudes from block 2.

## Headline: the friction curve cannot be extracted from this run

Mean lag needs the absolute command-to-encoder relationship to survive the
whole block. It did not. Of eleven plateaus:

- **5 lost position** - either the lag drifts by
  more than 40 counts across the plateau interior, or the
  apparent lag exceeds **one full step**. That bound is hard: one full step is
  90° electrical, the peak of the torque curve, and no stepper can hold a
  larger lag. Measured lags of 4.6°
  are therefore lost position, not friction.
- **4 more give a negative friction lag**, which is physically impossible:
  friction opposes motion, so the rotor lags its direction of travel and the
  forward-reverse difference must be positive. A negative value means the pair
  carries an offset bigger than the friction it is meant to measure.
- **3 survive both tests**: 0.25, 50, 100 full-step/s.

Three points, none below the Stribeck knee, cannot support a four-parameter
Stribeck + viscous fit, so none is reported. The direction-averaged offset,
which the brief uses as a check, reaches
**3.27°** where it should be near zero - the check
fails, and it fails for the same reason.

![Ripple magnification](fig5b_ripple_magnification.png)

**This half works.** Ripple is the residual after removing the mean lag, so a
constant position offset cancels out of it entirely. Each harmonic of each
plateau is divided by its static-map amplitude and plotted against
r = f_excitation / f_n, over the second-order magnification predicted by block 5

The 6 solid points track the predicted curve, rising
from 1.0 at low r towards resonance. That is an independent cross-validation of
block 5's f_n and zeta against data those parameters never saw.

## Reading these

- **Position is lost around the microstep-rate resonance.** The microstep rate
  is 16v, so it crosses f_n at **v = 9.6 full-step/s** -
  exactly where the 10 and 20 fs/s plateaus sit, and exactly where the drift is
  worst (+92, +250, -311 counts). This is classic stepper mid-band resonance,
  driven by the microstepping itself. The brief anticipated resonances where
  v/4, v/2 or v reaches f_n (612, 306, 153 fs/s), but the dominant forcing here
  is the 16v microstep rate, which reaches f_n two decades lower.
- **Below 10 fs/s the rotor does not follow the
  command at all**, it steps and sticks. Its "ripple" is the stepping, not the
  static error being magnified, which is why those points sit at 4-12x on
  Figure 5b. The brief flagged this; the data confirm it.
- **The high-speed lag rise is not viscous friction.** At 200 and 400 fs/s the
  apparent lag is 2.2° and 4.6°, which would need 100-200 mN·m against
  a holding torque near 50 mN·m. The motor would have stalled. These are
  accumulated slips, so no upper bound on sigma2 can be drawn from them either.
- **Ripple attenuation above resonance is real.** At 400 fs/s the 200/rev
  harmonic is excited at 400 Hz, r = 2.6, and its measured amplitude is 8% of
  the static value - the rotor simply cannot follow it. That plateau is flagged
  for step loss, but the attenuation is a residual-domain result and survives.

## What to change before re-running

1. **Bound the lag in firmware.** Log `encoder - command` each sample and abort
   the plateau if it exceeds half a full step. The run would have stopped at
   the first slip instead of producing eight contaminated plateaus.
2. **Ramp into and out of each plateau.** Every plateau currently starts at full
   rate from standstill; at 400 fs/s that is 6.4 kHz from rest, which is itself
   a likely slip source.
3. **Avoid or pass quickly through v near f_n/16.** For this motor that is
   10 fs/s, and it is the worst region in the block.
   The same applies to the stage: that speed band should be excluded from
   trajectory planning or crossed fast.
4. **Re-reference between plateaus.** The encoder index pulse arrives once per
   revolution; recording the command at each index crossing would let every
   plateau be re-zeroed and would have made this block recoverable.

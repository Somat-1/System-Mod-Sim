# Current sweep: separating torque-driven error from electrical error

Actions 17-25 of the 2026-10-13 identification run. At each of three current
levels: a 4 s settle, then four electrical cycles (256 native 1/16 microsteps,
28.8° of shaft) out and back as a staircase with 120 ms dwells.

Generated by [`../../scripts/current_sweep_figures.py`](../../scripts/current_sweep_figures.py).
Companion to [`../staircase/`](../staircase/), whose geometry fit this reuses.

## The currents are not 50 / 75 / 100 %

`IRUN` is a 5-bit code, so the requested milliamps quantise onto integer `CS`
values. CS = 9 at 360 mA is confirmed by the run's own register readback; the
other two follow from the same `TMCStepper` conversion.

| Requested | CS | Realised I_RMS | I₁₀₀ / I |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 180 mA | 4 | 166 mA | **2.00** |
| 270 mA | 7 | 265 mA | **1.25** |
| 360 mA | 9 | 331 mA | **1.00** |

So the x-axis of Figure 3b is **1.00, 1.25, 2.00**, not the 1, 1.33, 2 that the
requested currents would imply. This matters less than it looks: the ratios
come from the integer CS codes alone (5 : 8 : 10), so they are exact and do
**not** depend on `VSENSE`, `R_sense`, or the absolute current calibration.
Only the milliamp labels do.

## Figure 3a - Static error over one electrical cycle, at three currents

![Static error at three currents](fig3a_static_error_by_current.png)

Geometry-corrected, folded over the four electrical cycles of each level, with
each level's hysteresis band shaded. The geometry fit is taken from the static
map and **evaluated at absolute shaft angle** - it is never refitted here,
because 28.8° is not a whole revolution and a refit would absorb real motor
error.

Peak-to-peak grows from 0.088° at 331 mA to
0.197° at 166 mA - a factor
2.25 for a current ratio of 2.00 - and the band widens with it.
Both scale roughly as 1/I, which is the signature of torque-driven rather than
electrical error.

## Figure 3b - Amplitude against normalised inverse current

![Amplitude vs inverse current](fig3b_amplitude_vs_inverse_current.png)

Intercept at x = 0 is the current-independent (electrical) part; slope is the
torque-driven part. Error bars are 1σ, from the scatter the
three-harmonic model does not explain.

Two departures from the plan in the brief, both forced by the data:

1. **The fitted curves are not straight.** The brief assumed a straight line
   through the three amplitudes. Fitting the cos and sin parts separately - as
   the brief also asks - shows that for 100/rev the electrical and
   torque-driven parts sit **141°
   apart**, close to antiphase. Their magnitudes therefore partially cancel,
   and |A(I)| is not linear in 1/I. Fitting |A| directly returns a *negative*
   intercept, which is not a physical amplitude. The curves plotted are
   |a⃗ + b⃗x| from the vector fit, and the open marks at x = 0 are the
   true electrical magnitudes |a⃗|.
2. **Only 100/rev is resolved well enough to split.** With four cycles, 50/rev
   and 200/rev sit at SNR 2.3-4.1
   and 3.2-6.4. Their
   amplitudes are not even monotonic in current, so a two-parameter split is
   not determined. Their curves are drawn only across the measured range and
   carry no intercept mark.

### Reading these

- **Friction is textbook 1/I.** Hysteresis × I is
  13.08, 13.10, 13.52 °·mA - constant to
  3%. The free fit's intercept is
  0.0019° against a slope of
  0.0384°, i.e. indistinguishable from
  zero, and the through-origin fit holds to
  0.06 counts.
  With three levels and one parameter that is two degrees of freedom, so this
  is a real test and it passes. **h₀ ≈ 13.2°·mA.**
- **100/rev is mostly torque-driven, not waveform distortion.** It splits into
  0.0083° electrical and
  0.0314° torque-driven at
  nominal current - **67% torque-driven**. The model fits to
  within 0.01 counts on the
  cos part and 0.04 on the
  sin part, with one degree of freedom spare.

  This **corrects the reading in [`../staircase/`](../staircase/)**, which
  attributed the static map's 100/rev term to current-waveform distortion on
  the strength of its size alone. The current sweep shows most of it moves
  with 1/I. The two are consistent in magnitude: the static map gave
  0.0239° over a full revolution, and this block gives
  0.0258° at the same current over 28.8°.
- **The phase evidence supports the split.** 100/rev's total phase barely moves
  (5.8° across the three levels)
  against a per-level phase uncertainty of
  ~4°. That is what near-antiphase
  parts do: they change the magnitude strongly and the phase hardly at all.
  Quadrature parts would have rotated the phase instead.
- **Return error is +1 count at every level**, so no position was lost in this
  block. That matters, because the dynamics and reversal blocks later in the
  run did lose position.

## Checks

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Repeatability vs static map (100% level, same commanded positions) | rms **0.34 counts**, max 1.47, offset +0.03, over 255 positions |
| Settling at 120 ms, all three levels | tail spread 0.00 counts; see working plot |
| Ring frequency vs sqrt(I) | 117 / 150 / 167 Hz measured |
| Level differences, raw vs geometry-corrected | agree to 0.010 counts |

**Repeatability passes.** The brief expected the 100% level to reproduce the
matching section of the static map to about one count; it does so to
0.34 counts rms. These are independent measurements taken
~14 minutes apart, at the same commanded positions, so this bounds the
short-term repeatability of the whole settled-staircase method.

**The settling re-check was worth doing.** The brief predicted
ωₙ about 0.7× lower at 50% current. Measured ring frequencies
are 117, 150 and 167 Hz, giving
0.70 and 0.90 against
0.71 and
0.89 predicted from
sqrt(I). Every tail is settled to 0.00 counts, so 120 ms holds at 50% current
as well. (500 Hz sampling makes the FFT bins ~17 Hz wide, so these frequencies
are coarse; the agreement should not be read as better than about 10%.)

**The cross-check is a bookkeeping check, not a validation of the geometry
fit.** Subtracting one level from another at identical commanded positions
cancels the geometry term algebraically, whichever fit is used - that is
exactly why the brief suggests it. The 0.010
count agreement therefore confirms that the two folding conventions (pair-then-
fold versus fold-then-pair) match; it does not independently confirm that the
static map's 1/rev + 2/rev term is really encoder geometry. What it does
guarantee is that **every level-to-level difference in this analysis is
independent of the geometry correction.**

## Method notes

- Each direction is folded over **its own** 256-microstep window, which is
  exactly four electrical cycles, so all 64 phases get exactly 4 samples.
  Pairing the two directions first would leave 255 points and give phase 0 only
  3 samples.
- Harmonics are fitted on the folded cycle at electrical orders 1, 2 and 4
  (= 50, 100, 200 per revolution). The folded cycle is exactly periodic, so the
  three orders are mutually orthogonal and cannot leak into one another.
- Standard errors treat everything the three-harmonic model leaves as noise.
  That is conservative: the residual also holds real structure at other orders,
  so a term clearing this bar is genuinely resolved.
- Settled values are the mean of the final 28%
  of each 120 ms dwell, as in the static map.
- Levels use an ordinal blue ramp (sequential steps 300/500/700); the three
  harmonic orders use validated categorical slots 1-3, and the hysteresis wears
  ink, which needs no hue validation.

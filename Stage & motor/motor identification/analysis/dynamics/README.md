# Block 5 - Dynamics: natural frequency, damping, and absolute torque

Actions 26-45 of the 2026-10-13 identification run. At four rotor angles (0,
50, 100, 150 full steps), twelve out-and-back single-step excitations at
MRES=1 (one full step, 1.8°) and twelve at MRES=16 (one microstep,
0.1125°), both directions, 350 ms dwells, sampled at
512 us. Both the excitation and the
reset edge are usable responses, giving 184 full-step and
184 microstep responses.

Generated by [`../../scripts/dynamics_figures.py`](../../scripts/dynamics_figures.py).

## Headline: most full-step responses are unusable, and that is the result

A full step is **90° electrical**. The measured overshoot is
**79%**, which puts the rotor's first peak at about
**161° electrical** - just short of
the 180° pull-out angle where the restoring torque reverses. The rotor is
therefore marginally stable on every full step, and only
**12 of 184** responses settle one step away. The
other 172 carry on into the adjacent equilibrium and travel
1.94 full steps for a one-step command.

The clean ones are all at the start of action 26, before the rotor's resting
offset had drifted; see
[`working/w7_settled_position.png`](working/w7_settled_position.png). Every
number below that depends on the full-step ringdown comes from those
12 responses only. The microstep responses are unaffected - they
are genuinely small-signal - and all 184 are used.

**This is a campaign design problem, not a data-processing one.** A one-full-step
excitation is too large to be a clean ringdown test on this motor. A
two-microstep or four-microstep excitation would stay well inside the linear
region and give the same f_n without risking pull-out.

### Absolute scale needs one number this repo does not have

f_n fixes only the **ratio** K/J. Turning it into torque needs J, and the
encoder disc and hub on this bench were never weighed. Three readings:

| Assumption | J [kg·m²] | K_theta [N·m/rad] | T_hold [mN·m] | T_f [mN·m] |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Rotor inertia alone (datasheet 9.0e-07) | 9.00e-07 | 0.832 | 16.6 | 0.64 |
| Nameplate 0.060 N·m read as **RMS** at 400 mA | 2.69e-06 | 2.486 | 49.7 | 1.92 |
| Nameplate read as **peak** at 400 mA | 3.80e-06 | 3.516 | 70.3 | 2.71 |

Reading down the first row: if J were the rotor alone, T_hold would be
16.6 mN·m, which is
3.0x
below the nameplate scaled to the realised 331.5 mA. Read the other way, the
nameplate implies a total J of
2.69e-06-3.80e-06 kg·m²,
i.e. **3.0 to
4.2 times the rotor
alone**. An encoder disc and hub of two to three times the rotor inertia is
entirely plausible, so the measurement and the datasheet are consistent - but
the split cannot be closed without weighing the disc.

**Weigh the encoder disc and hub, or measure f_n with the encoder removed.**
That single number converts every relative result in this campaign into N·m.

## Reading these

- **f_n = 153 Hz is solid.** Two independent routes agree: the
  full-step ringdown fit (153.0 Hz) and the microstep first-peak
  timing (156 Hz), which share no fitted parameters.
- **Damping is viscous, not Coulomb - against expectation.** The decay envelope
  is exponential with R² 0.996 against
  0.792 for a straight line
  ([`working/w6_decay_envelope.png`](working/w6_decay_envelope.png)). A Coulomb
  envelope would be linear. For the LuGre work this matters: during a fast
  ringdown the energy loss is dominated by a velocity-proportional term, not by
  the friction model.
- **Both are true at once, at different amplitudes.** zeta is
  0.053 for the full step and 0.74 for the
  microstep. A fixed Coulomb torque is negligible against the restoring torque
  at 90° electrical but dominant at 5.6°, so the apparent damping is two
  orders of magnitude apart. Any model fitted to only one amplitude will
  mispredict the other.
- **The free three-parameter fit is ill-conditioned.** Letting zeta and the
  Coulomb band both float lands at zeta 0.0055 with a
  0.0813° band - a Coulomb-dominated solution that cannot
  reproduce the late ringing, at a *worse* residual
  (0.0397 vs 0.0327). The reported fit
  takes zeta from the envelope, which is model-free, and fits the remaining two.
- **Microstep travel varies 0.84-1.64 with
  rotor angle.** A microstep command moves the equilibrium by 6.25 counts; the
  rotor moves between 84% and 164% of that depending where it sits in the
  electrical cycle. That is the detent torque modulating the effective
  stiffness, and its size is consistent with the 5 mN·m detent in the repo's
  model parameters against a ~50 mN·m holding torque.
- **The f_n spread over four angles is an upper bound, not a measurement.** At
  45% it is far above the few percent expected. It comes
  from first-peak timing on traces quantised to one encoder count, where one
  sample of timing error moves f_n by tens of percent. The travel-ratio route
  above is the better evidence for stiffness variation.
- **Final-position scatter is 0.074° against
  0.044° from the static map** - same order,
  from twelve responses. The consistency check across blocks passes, loosely.

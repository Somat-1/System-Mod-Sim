# Staircase: what the encoder measured

Two figures and one table from actions 15 and 16 of the 2026-10-13
identification run - every native 1/16 microstep over one shaft revolution
forward, then the identical commanded positions in reverse, with a 150 ms
settling dwell at each.

Generated by [`../../scripts/staircase_figures.py`](../../scripts/staircase_figures.py).
What was commanded is documented in
[`../../COMMANDED_SEQUENCE.md`](../../COMMANDED_SEQUENCE.md).

## Figure 1 - Error over one revolution

![Error over one shaft revolution](fig1_error_over_revolution.png)

Encoder angle minus commanded angle against shaft angle for both directions,
with the fitted 1/rev + 2/rev curve dashed on top.

The slow wave is encoder eccentricity; the fine ripple riding on it is the
motor. The geometry error is **4.5x larger** than the motor's own
error, and the two fitted orders account for 99.2%
of the variance in the direction-pair mean. The fit is taken on the mean of the
two passes so that hysteresis cannot bias it, and no linear term is included -
nothing but true geometry orders is removed.

## The geometry fit itself

![The geometry fit](geometry_fit.png)

The correction every later block subtracts, rendered on its own:
[`geometry_fit.png`](geometry_fit.png), with coefficients in
[`geometry_fit.json`](geometry_fit.json) and the curve sampled in
[`geometry_fit_curve.csv`](geometry_fit_curve.csv). In encoder counts the fit is

    geo(theta) = -10.6227 + 7.7769 cos(theta) - 19.8947 sin(theta)
                          - 0.0095 cos(2 theta) - 0.1604 sin(2 theta)

The 2/rev term is 0.8% of the 1/rev term, so the correction is a single
sinusoid of 21.36 counts at phase 291.4 degrees. The constant is an arbitrary
offset that cancels out of every difference taken in this campaign.

The right-hand panel is why it is fitted once over a whole revolution and never
refitted locally: across the 28.8 degrees a current level spans, the curve
departs from a straight line by **0.07 counts**. A local refit would therefore
be indistinguishable from an offset plus a slope, and would quietly absorb
motor error into the geometry term.

## Figure 2 - The motor's error over one electrical cycle

![Motor error over one electrical cycle](fig2_electrical_cycle.png)

The eccentricity fit subtracted, then folded onto one electrical cycle
(0-7.2° = 4 full steps) and averaged over all 50 cycles. Forward and reverse
are drawn separately with the gap between them shaded; light vertical lines
mark the 4 full-step positions.

- **The shape of the curve** is the repeatable microstep error - where the
  rotor really sits for each commanded microstep.
- **The width of the shaded band** is the friction hysteresis - how much the
  result depends on the direction you arrived from.

Averaging 50 cycles puts the standard error at
0.088 counts (median), about 1.8% of the
peak-to-peak shape, so the structure in this curve is real and not sampling
noise. Underlying values are in
[`fig2_electrical_cycle.csv`](fig2_electrical_cycle.csv).

## The numbers

| Quantity | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Eccentricity amplitude (1/rev) | **0.3845°** (21.36 counts, 1384 arcsec), phase 291° | Encoder geometry error. Cross-check below. |
| 2/rev amplitude | 0.0029° (0.16 counts) | Negligible; the geometry term is almost pure 1/rev. |
| Peak-to-peak static error | **0.0860°** (310 arcsec) = **4.78% of a full step** | The motor's microstep accuracy, after removing geometry. |
| Mean hysteresis (half band width) | **0.0442°** (159 arcsec) = 2.45% of a full step | Friction / stiffness. Range over the cycle 2.14-2.75 counts. |
| 50/rev amplitude | 0.0135° (49 arcsec) | Phase-current mismatch between the two windings. |
| 100/rev amplitude | **0.0239°** (86 arcsec) | The largest motor term. Mostly torque-driven, not waveform distortion — see the correction below. |
| 200/rev amplitude | 0.0098° (35 arcsec) | Detent torque, at the full-step positions. |
| Counts per revolution | **20001.2 +/- 6.0** over 60 index intervals | Scale check against the nominal 20,000. Passes. |
| Start vs end drift | staircase **-2.5 counts** (-162 arcsec); whole run +267 counts | Repeatability. The staircase closes; the run as a whole does not. |

### Reading these

- **Geometry dominates, and it is not the motor's fault.** At
  0.3845° the 1/rev term swamps everything else. Treating
  the raw trace as motor error would overstate it by about 4x.
- **The eccentricity cross-checks against the v1 dial measurement.** That run
  measured 159.5 um of radial runout. For an eccentric disc the angular error
  amplitude is approximately e/R, so 0.3845° implies a
  scale radius of **23.8 mm** - a physically
  plausible disc for this encoder. Consistent, though not a proof: the repo
  still lacks the measured scale radius, phase registration, and a common
  clock, so the dial result is not applied as an angle calibration.
- **100/rev is the largest motor term**, at 86 arcsec against 49 arcsec for
  50/rev and 35 arcsec for 200/rev.

  > **Corrected by the current sweep.** This section originally read
  > "waveform distortion outweighs both current mismatch and detent", naming
  > 100/rev as current-waveform distortion on the strength of its size alone.
  > Size alone cannot establish that. [`../current_sweep/`](../current_sweep/)
  > varies the current and finds 100/rev is **about two-thirds torque-driven**:
  > 0.0083° current-independent against 0.0314° scaling as 1/I at nominal
  > current. The magnitudes agree across the two blocks (0.0239° here over a
  > full revolution, 0.0258° there at the same current over 28.8°), so the
  > measurement stands — only its attribution changes. Note also that the
  > realised currents were 0.50 / 0.80 / 1.00 of nominal rather than the
  > intended 0.50 / 0.75 / 1.00; see the quantisation caveat in
  > `COMMANDED_SEQUENCE.md`.
- **Settled hysteresis is small, and much smaller than the microstep-resolution
  run suggests.** 2.45 counts here, against 16-35
  counts in the 1/64 and 1/128 revolutions of the 16:47 run. That run used
  2-3 ms dwells, so the rotor never settled and what it recorded was dynamic
  lag, not static friction hysteresis. The two numbers measure different things.
- **The staircase closes to -2.5 counts**, well
  inside one microstep (6.25 counts). The whole run's
  +267 counts accumulated later, in the dynamics
  and reversal blocks; the staircase itself is clean.

## Method notes

- 3,199 commanded positions are shared by both
  passes (one microstep short of the full 3,200, since the two passes stop at
  opposite ends).
- Each settled value is the mean of the final 28%
  of its 150 ms dwell, about 21 samples of the
  76 recorded.
- Harmonic amplitudes are least-squares projections at the exact mechanical
  orders, not FFT bins. The common command grid is one microstep short of a
  whole revolution, so an FFT would leak; the spectrum working plot is
  FFT-based and is indicative only.
- Colours are slots 1 and 2 of the validated categorical palette, used
  unchanged. Figures are light-surface only, as report artefacts.

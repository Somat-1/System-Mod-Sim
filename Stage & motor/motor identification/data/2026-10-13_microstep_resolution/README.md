# Microstep resolution test — 2026-10-13

How finely does the motor step at the fine end? One full shaft revolution
forward and back in single microsteps, at 1/32, 1/64 and 1/128, plus ±1
microstep responses. The plan is `plan.csv` (generated by
`../../scripts/microstep_resolution_design.py`): 13 actions, 4.75 min. The run
started 16:47 local and completed (`RUN_COMPLETE`) in 4 min 55 s.

## Results (`scripts/microstep_resolution_analysis.py`)

| MRES | ideal step | mean step | encoder over the rev fwd / back | hysteresis | ±1 response |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1/32 | 3.125 counts | 3.195 | **+20,335 / −20,572** | (see below) | 108 % of ideal |
| 1/64 | 1.5625 | 1.560 | +19,996 / −19,958 | −35 counts (−0.63°) | 64 % |
| 1/128 | 0.78125 | 0.780 | +19,984 / −19,973 | −16 counts (−0.28°) | 27 % |

- **1/64 and 1/128:**
  - The average step size is correct to within 0.2%.
  - Over a revolution the error stays within about ±40 counts, in a smooth
    once-per-revolution shape, which suggests encoder eccentricity rather than
    the motor.
  - The rotor lags the command in the direction of travel by roughly
    0.15–0.3 full steps, which shows up as the hysteresis. After a reversal,
    roughly that many microsteps pass before the rotor follows.
- **Single microsteps get progressively less effective.** One microstep moves
  the rotor by its full amount at 1/32, about two-thirds of it at 1/64 and about
  a quarter at 1/128 (averaged over 10 ±1 repeats). At 1/128 one microstep is
  under one encoder count, so that figure is from averaging and is approximate.
- **Step spread** (42–84%) at these sizes is mostly encoder quantisation (one
  count is 1/3 to 1 1/4 of a step), not motor behaviour.

## Open issue: the 1/32 revolution

At 1/32 the encoder recorded 20,335 counts forward and −20,572 back, against
±20,000 commanded. The error grew gradually: +157 counts within the first
1/16 revolution, then steadily to +335. There was no single jump (no step over
40 counts). On the way back it reached −576.

A stepper locked to its field cannot rotate gradually *further* than commanded
in both directions. It loses or gains position in jumps, normally of whole
electrical cycles (400 counts). So this looks like the **encoder over-counting**
rather than the motor. Candidates:

- quadrature ringing at each 1/32 step, which is the largest step of the three
  and has the shortest settling before the next one, being miscounted;
- a mechanical slip in the encoder coupling.

1/64 and 1/128, run straight after with the same wiring, are clean. **Treat the
1/32 position data as suspect until a repeat** (`--mres 32`, about 1.6 min)
shows whether it reproduces. Its ±1 response is a local measurement and is
unaffected.
